Title: Quantum Computing and the Future of Cryptography

Abstract:
Quantum computing poses a significant threat to modern cryptography. This paper explores the fundamentals of quantum mechanics, specifically superposition and entanglement, and how they empower Shor's algorithm to break RSA encryption. We propose post-quantum cryptographic methods, including lattice-based and hash-based cryptography, as robust alternatives for securing future communications.

1. Introduction
Modern secure communication relies on the difficulty of factoring large prime numbers, a problem RSA encryption depends on. Classical computers would take billions of years to break RSA-2048. However, a sufficiently powerful quantum computer could break it in hours. 

2. Quantum Mechanics in Computing
Quantum computers use qubits, which can exist in a state of superposition (representing 0 and 1 simultaneously). When combined with quantum entanglement, qubits can perform parallel computations at a scale classical bits cannot match.

3. Shor's Algorithm
Developed by Peter Shor in 1994, this quantum algorithm can find the prime factors of an integer exponentially faster than any known classical algorithm. If a stable quantum computer is built, Shor's algorithm will render RSA obsolete.

4. Post-Quantum Cryptography
To prepare for "Q-Day", NIST has been evaluating post-quantum cryptography (PQC) algorithms. Lattice-based cryptography, which relies on the Shortest Vector Problem (SVP), is currently the leading candidate. Hash-based signatures, such as XMSS, also offer quantum-resistant security but are limited by their stateful nature.

Conclusion:
The transition to quantum-safe encryption is not optional. Organizations must begin adopting lattice-based cryptography today to protect against future "harvest now, decrypt later" attacks.
